How to write a blog post

By The First-Year Workshop·20 October 2026

Here's how to write a blog post for the First-Year Workshop, and what one looks like on the page.

The blog is a place for first-year PhD students in Econ, ARE, and Haas to write short pieces for each other: an idea you're working on, a paper you liked, a method you've just figured out, or something you've learned this year. Posts run from a few hundred words up to 1,000.

Good topics include research you're working on, a review of a paper you've read closely, something useful you've learned, notes on the papers at a seminar or conference you attended (kept to the research), a dataset or method worth knowing about, or an open question you think deserves a paper.

Write for a PhD student in any of the three programs, not just your own field. If a term or a piece of notation isn't common across Econ, ARE, and Haas, explain it. One clear point is better than three rushed ones.

To send a post in, use the form at https://berkeley-firstyearworkshop.github.io/blog/submit.html. You'll need your name, email, program, a title, and your text. Your program is for us and isn't published. Your email appears at the end of your post so readers can get in touch, unless you check "Hide my email on the blog post."

Write in plain text and leave a blank line between paragraphs. Posts go up as written, so proofread before you send yours.

Math is welcome, written in LaTeX, and it doesn't count toward the word limit. For inline math, use single dollar signs: typing $\beta R < 1$ gives \(\beta R < 1\). For a displayed equation, use double dollar signs on a line of their own: typing $$u'(c_t) = \beta R \, \mathbb{E}_t[u'(c_{t+1})]$$ gives

\[u'(c_t) = \beta R \, \mathbb{E}_t\left[u'(c_{t+1})\right]\]

Dollar amounts are fine as they are, so a $500 travel grant stays as text.

To link to a paper or website, put the address in square brackets right after the reference, starting with "link:". The reference becomes the link, and links don't count toward the word limit either. The submission form shows an example.

You can add one image. It goes at the top of your post and is its thumbnail on the blog page. It must be your own photo or research figure, or licensed for reuse (Unsplash, Pexels, CC0 or public domain, or CC BY with credit), and you need to say where it comes from. Images from Google Images or news sites, figures from published journal articles, and logos aren't allowed. Without an image, your post gets the banner at the top of this one.

You keep the copyright to your post; by sending it in, you let us publish it here. New posts go out to the mailing list on Sunday evenings. To ask for a post or an image to be removed, email berkeleyfirstyearworkshop@gmail.com.
